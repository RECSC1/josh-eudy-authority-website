New Construction & Builder Representation

Do I need my own agent to buy new construction?

No, but you want one, and it costs you nothing. The agent in the builder's model home is paid by the builder and represents the builder. Builder pricing does not drop because you came alone. What changes is who is reading the contract on your side of the table.

Can I add an agent after I have already visited the model home?

Usually not. Most builders in this market require your broker to be with you or named at your first registration, and they will not honor representation added later. If you are even thinking about touring a model, bring your broker to the first visit or call them before you sign the visitor card.

What can you actually negotiate with a builder?

Rarely the base price, because a recorded price cut hurts every other home in the community. What does move is closing cost assistance through the builder's preferred lender, design center allowances, lot premiums, included upgrades, and the completion timeline. Ask what is sitting finished and unsold, because that is where the real concessions live.

Is the builder's contract the same as a normal North Carolina offer?

No. Builders write their own contracts and they are not the standard NC Offer to Purchase. Deposits are often larger and less refundable, the arbitration and warranty language is theirs, and the completion date may be far softer than you think. Read it with someone who has read a hundred of them.

What should happen at the walkthroughs?

Two of them. A pre drywall walkthrough while the framing, wiring and plumbing are still visible, which is the only chance you get to see the bones. Then a final walkthrough with a written punch list, item by item, signed by the builder before closing. Verbal promises at a final walkthrough are worth nothing.

Building in Holly Ridge or Sneads Ferry, where most of the new inventory is? Bring me in before you sign the builder contract. More on how I represent buyers.

Military Relocation & PCS Moves

Should I buy or rent when I PCS to Camp Lejeune?

It depends almost entirely on your tour length and your tolerance for being a landlord. Under three years, renting usually wins once you account for closing costs on both ends. Three years or more, buying often wins, especially with a VA loan and no down payment. The honest version of this math includes what the house would rent for if you get orders early, because in this market a lot of people end up holding.

Can I buy a house here without ever seeing it?

Yes, and it is a large share of the business in Onslow County. Video walkthroughs on your schedule, a local inspector who tells you the truth, and a broker who will walk the neighborhood at the hour you would actually be driving it. The risk is not the house. The risk is the street, the drainage and the commute, which no listing photo shows.

How much house will my BAH actually cover in Jacksonville?

BAH is set by rank, dependency status and zip, and it does not track this market in real time. Run your specific rate against a real payment including taxes and current insurance, not a mortgage calculator's default. Insurance is the line that surprises people here, particularly anywhere south or east toward the water.

Which gate should I live near?

Pick the neighborhood after you pick the gate, not before. Sneads Ferry Gate and Wilson Gate run 24 hours. Piney Green Gate runs inbound weekdays only, roughly 0530 to 1700, with a short outbound extension. The TOP Gate on NC-172, which comes out on Bear Creek Road in Hubert, is on reduced hours and closed weekends and holidays. Gates also close for construction and for fog. A commute that only works through one gate is a commute with a single point of failure.

How early should I start if my report date is six months out?

Start looking at six months, get pre approved at ninety days, and plan to be under contract around forty five days out. Earlier than that and you are shopping inventory that will be gone. Later and you are choosing from whatever is left in the worst week of your year.

Orders came early and I own the house. Now what?

You get both answers from one conversation. What it would list for and how long that realistically takes, against what it would rent for and what the carrying cost actually is. Josh buys and holds property in this county himself, so the rental math comes from someone running it, not theorizing about it. If the right answer is sell, he will say sell.

Start with buy or rent for a Lejeune move, then decide between buying and renting first. Town by town detail is on the areas page.

Home Prep, Staging & Renovation

What should I actually fix before I sell?

The things a buyer notices in the first eight seconds and the things an inspector will find anyway. Paint, flooring if it is bad, pressure washing, landscaping, lighting and anything that leaks or does not close. These return more in this price band than a kitchen does.

What should I not fix?

Full kitchen and bath renovations, on a ninety day timeline, almost never return what they cost here. Neither does replacing a system that still works just because it is old. If the roof is at the end of its life, price for it rather than replace it, and say so up front so it does not become a renegotiation later.

Can I just sell it as is?

Yes, and sometimes you should. As is means priced for condition, not hidden condition, and North Carolina still requires the Residential Property Disclosure. If the work is beyond what you want to take on, the comparison is a discounted list price against a direct cash offer, and you are entitled to see both numbers before deciding.

Does staging actually make a difference?

In occupied homes, decluttering and furniture removal do most of the work and cost almost nothing. Full professional staging earns its fee mainly on vacant homes and higher price points, because empty rooms photograph small and buyers cannot judge scale. Below the county median it is usually not worth the invoice.

How much should I plan to spend?

Most sellers in Onslow County land somewhere between a few hundred dollars and a few thousand, and the ones who spend the most rarely net the most. You get a written punch list in priority order with a real number next to each line and a clear note on which items to skip. Spend top down and stop when the next item stops paying for itself.

Prep spend only makes sense against the number you walk away with. Run it through the net proceeds calculator, then see the full listing plan.

Photography, Video & Listing Media

Do professional photos really matter?

Your listing gets roughly one week of genuine attention, and nearly every buyer meets it on a phone screen first. The photos decide whether anyone books a showing. This is the cheapest leverage in the entire transaction and it is the one most commonly skipped.

Who pays for the photography?

Josh does, on every listing, through a local photographer who shoots this market. It is not an add on, a tier, or a line item you are billed for. Ask any agent quoting you a listing fee whether media is included, because frequently it is not.

Do I need drone or video?

Not every house. Drone earns its place on waterfront, acreage, land and anything where the lot is the story. Video earns its place on homes with a layout that photos flatten, and on anything a relocating buyer will decide on remotely, which here is a large share of buyers.

What happens to the photos after my house sells?

This is worth asking every agent you interview, because the answer is often not what you expect. Listing photos are typically licensed to the brokerage for marketing that listing, not owned by you. If you want to keep and reuse them afterward, get it in writing before the shoot.

Great photos are on the MLS. Is that enough?

No, and this is where most listings quietly fail. The media has to land everywhere at once: the MLS, syndication that actually completes rather than half posting, a property page written to be found in search, a Google Business Profile post the day it goes live, and direct distribution to local agents and past clients. Beautiful photos sitting in one place are a wasted invoice.

Media is one piece of the listing. The rest of it is on how I sell a house, and you can see finished work on the portfolio.

Mortgage & Lending Partners

Do I need to be pre approved before I start looking?

Before you write an offer, yes. Before you look, no. Go see houses. But start the paperwork early, because if you fall in love with something in week one without a pre approval, you will lose it to someone who has one.

Why do VA offers sometimes lose to weaker offers?

Because listing agents fear the appraisal and the minimum property requirements, and many of them cannot distinguish between a VA buyer and a VA problem. A large share of this market runs on VA loans, so the fix is routine here: a local lender who will call the listing agent directly and a pre approval letter that reads as real. Presented properly, a VA offer competes.

Should I wait for rates to come down?

Run the actual numbers on the house you want rather than on a hypothetical. Waiting costs you rent, the house you wanted, and sometimes a price increase that outweighs the rate improvement. Rates can be refinanced later. The house you lost cannot.

Can I buy before I sell?

Often yes, through a bridge loan, a recast, or a contingent offer, and each carries a different risk if your current house takes longer than you expect. In Onslow County right now, time on market is the variable that has moved, so build the plan around a slower sale rather than your best case.

Does Josh get paid by the lenders he recommends?

No. He takes no referral fee and no kickback from any lender, which is also what federal law requires. The list is short because it is built on who answers the phone on a Sunday and who closes on time, not on who pays.

Before you talk to anyone, get a realistic number from the payment and affordability calculator. Then how I represent buyers.

Closing Attorney & Title

Do I need a lawyer to buy a house in North Carolina?

Yes. North Carolina is an attorney closing state. A licensed attorney handles the title search, the title insurance commitment, the deed and the disbursement of funds. If you are moving from a title company state like Texas or Florida, this is the first structural difference you will hit.

What is the Due Diligence Fee and is it refundable?

The Due Diligence Fee is paid directly to the seller when the contract is signed, in exchange for the right to terminate for any reason during the Due Diligence Period. Under standard Form 2-T, Paragraph 1(i), it is non refundable except in narrow cases: the seller materially breaches, the buyer terminates under Paragraph 8 Seller Obligations or Paragraph 12 Risk of Loss, or an addendum specifically provides for a refund. The North Carolina Real Estate Commission describes actual refunds as rare. If the sale closes, the fee is credited to you.

How is Earnest Money different?

Earnest Money is held in escrow, not paid to the seller, and it is refundable if you terminate before the Due Diligence Period expires. After that deadline passes, the seller can keep it. Two payments, same contract, completely different risk. Understand both before you sign.

When does title work start?

The day you go under contract, not the week of closing. A boundary issue, an unreleased mortgage, an old lien or an heirs property problem takes time to clear, and finding it late is the single most common reason closing dates move in this county.

How long does closing take here?

Thirty to forty five days is typical for a financed purchase, faster for cash. The timeline is driven by the lender and the title work, not by the attorney. If you are on a PCS report date, say so at contract, because the whole schedule can be built backward from it.

North Carolina is an attorney closing state, which catches out-of-state buyers constantly. It matters on both sides: buying and selling.

Leasing & Rental Placement

Should I sell my house or rent it out when I leave?

Start with the real number, not the hopeful one. What it leases for today in that specific neighborhood, against the mortgage, taxes, current insurance, maintenance and realistic vacancy. In this market the insurance line is what breaks most spreadsheets, especially anywhere coastal. If it clears, holding can be the better long term move. If it does not, selling is not a failure.

What will my house actually rent for?

Benchmarked against what is leasing right now in that neighborhood, not what is listed. Listed rents include the ones sitting empty at the wrong price. Jacksonville rents move with the base calendar, so the month you put it on matters nearly as much as the number.

Do you handle ongoing property management?

No, and you should be told that clearly rather than discovering it later. Josh handles rent benchmarking, tenant placement, screening and the lease. For full ongoing management he will refer you to people he trusts and tell you honestly where the line is.

What do most first time landlords here get wrong?

Three things. They use last year's insurance figure instead of a current quote. They budget zero for vacancy in a market where tenants rotate with orders. And they screen loosely because the first applicant arrived quickly. One bad placement costs more than a year of the rent premium they were chasing.

Can I rent it out and still sell later?

Yes, and the lease terms decide how easy that is. A lease running past the window you might want to sell in limits your buyer pool to investors, who pay less. If selling within a year or two is possible, structure the lease around that from the start.

If you are renting first and buying later, that is a real strategy here, not a fallback. See rentals and rent before you buy, and the town by town guide for where to land.

Investors, Flips & 1031

Does the rent math still work in Onslow County?

On some deals, yes, and fewer than two years ago. The county median sat near $311,000 for the three months ending September 2026 per Redfin, and insurance has moved sharply on anything coastal. Deals still pencil inland and on manufactured and mobile homes. Oceanward, the carrying cost eats the spread. Run current insurance quotes, not last year's.

What are the 1031 deadlines?

Forty five days from closing to identify replacement property in writing, and one hundred eighty days to close on it. Both clocks start at the sale closing and neither is flexible. Engage your qualified intermediary before you sell, because you cannot touch the proceeds. People lose exchanges on paperwork, not on property.

Can you handle manufactured and mobile homes?

Yes, including park owned and land home packages, and Josh owns a mobile home park himself. Most agents in this market avoid these because the financing, titling and land ownership questions are different from stick built. Whether the home is real property or personal property changes the entire transaction.

What should I know before buying land here?

Perc test, wetlands and access, in that order. A pretty lot with no septic approval is not a buildable lot, it is a hunting tract with a view. In eastern North Carolina wetlands delineation can remove most of the usable acreage from a parcel that looks fine from the road. Make the offer contingent on all three.

Will you tell me when a deal is bad?

That is most of the value. Josh is buying in the same county with his own money, which means he is not incentivized to talk you into a property he would not buy himself. If the numbers do not work, you will hear that before you hear a pitch.

I buy as well as list, so you can see the inventory side on the portfolio, or get a cash offer and a list price on something you already own.

Inherited, Land & Problem Properties

Can I sell a house I inherited in North Carolina?

Usually yes, but not always immediately, and this is where most people get stuck. Title to real property generally vests in the heirs or devisees at death, so there may be no need to wait for a full administration. However, under Chapter 28A, sales within two years of death remain exposed to estate creditor claims, so the personal representative often has to join the deed and a title insurer may require probate steps before it will insure. The first call here is frequently the clerk of superior court or an estate attorney, not a Realtor.

What if there are several heirs and we do not all agree?

Common, and solvable, but start early. Everyone with an interest generally has to sign. If one heir will not, the remaining options are buying that interest out or a partition action through the court, which is slow and expensive. Get everyone on one call before the property is listed, not after an offer arrives.

Should I take a cash offer or list it?

You should see both numbers before you decide, and almost nobody in this situation is shown both. A listing gets a higher gross price and takes longer, with showings, repairs and carrying costs on a house nobody lives in. A direct cash offer is lower and closes fast, as is, with no cleanout. Which one nets more depends on condition, timeline and how far away you live.

Do I have to clean it out first?

Not for a direct purchase. Josh buys properties with the contents still in them, which matters when the house is three states away and full of forty years of belongings. For a traditional listing, yes, it has to be cleared and that cost belongs in your comparison.

What if I am behind on payments or facing foreclosure?

Act sooner than feels comfortable, because options narrow fast as the sale date approaches. Depending on equity and timeline, that can mean a fast list, a direct purchase, or a conversation with the lender you have not had yet. There is usually more room than people think at ninety days out and very little at thirty.

What about a lot or land I will never build on?

Land is its own market and most agents treat it as an afterthought. Value turns on perc test results, wetlands, road frontage and access. Josh buys lots and land directly as well as listing them, so you get a real number either way rather than a listing that sits for a year.
