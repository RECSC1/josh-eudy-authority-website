Jacksonville, NC

What does a house actually cost in Jacksonville?

Redfin puts the median sale at $250,000 for the three months ending September 2026, up 4.1 percent, around $176 a foot, selling in about 52 days. Onslow County over the same window ran $311,000. Both numbers are real and they describe different places, which is why a page quoting one Jacksonville figure without saying city or county is useless to you. Sixty thousand dollars of difference decides whether a listing looks like a deal. Price a specific house off recent closed sales within a mile of it, not off a headline median for a geography you are not buying in.

Which Camp Lejeune gate should I live near?

Pick the gate before the neighborhood. People do this backwards constantly, fall in love with a house, then discover the entrance they are authorized for adds twenty five minutes. Sneads Ferry Gate and Wilson Gate are listed as open 24 hours. Piney Green runs weekdays only. The TOP Gate on NC-172 is on reduced hours with weekend and holiday closures, and the published schedules for it have changed more than once, so confirm current access with the Provost Marshal's Office rather than trusting any directory, mine included. Then drive your real route at your real report time before you make an offer.

Can I negotiate on a Jacksonville house right now?

You can ask, and the evidence on the specific listing decides whether you get it. Fifty two days on market with prices up 4.1 percent is not a desperate market and it is not a seller's market either. It is a market where overpriced houses sit and correctly priced ones move. Look at the price history first. A listing with two reductions and ninety days behind it is a different conversation from one that came on last Thursday. Then choose your ask deliberately: price, repairs, or closing costs. Asking for all three at once on a fresh listing is how buyers get ignored.

Is Jacksonville only worth it if I am military?

No, though the base sets the rhythm whether you serve or not. Inventory turns on orders rather than seasons and the buyer pool refreshes every summer, which means there is always a buyer and always another house. The city has a river running through it that most people never use. Riverwalk Crossing Park and the marina downtown put the New River within reach without driving to the coast, Jacksonville Commons handles the recreation most families actually need, and Lejeune Memorial Gardens explains the place better than any tour would. It is a working city, not a destination, and it does not pretend otherwise.

What is actually changing in this market?

Not price. Time. Prices held through 2026 while days on market stretched, and that shift moves the whole burden onto pricing and preparation. Two years ago a seller could be wrong by fifteen thousand dollars and the market would absorb it in a week. Now being wrong by fifteen thousand costs you forty days and a price reduction that signals weakness to everybody watching. Sellers who price correctly in the first week still do fine. Sellers who price on what their neighbor got in 2024 are the ones calling me in month three.

What should I check that most buyers skip here?

Flood. Not just the coastal towns, Jacksonville too, because the New River and its creeks reach further into neighborhoods than people expect and the maps have been redrawn. Pull the flood determination on the actual parcel before you write, not during due diligence when your fee is already with the seller. Second thing: if the house is near a gate route, drive past it at shift change. A street that is quiet at two in the afternoon can be a queue at six in the morning, and no listing photograph will tell you that.

If you are weighing a sale here, the net proceeds calculator will show you what actually lands in your account, and the listing plan explains how I price a Jacksonville house against orders rather than seasons. PCS buyers should start with buy or rent for a Lejeune move.

Sneads Ferry, NC

Why does every website show a different price for Sneads Ferry?

Because they are counting different things and almost nobody says so. Redfin: $308,000 median sale, three months ending September 2026, down 3.5 percent. Realtor.com: $420,000 median listing. Zillow: $374,019 estimated value. Resideline: $312,000 across 69 closed sales. That is a $112,000 spread on one town. Asking prices are what sellers hope for, closed sales are what buyers paid, and an automated value is a model's guess. If you are selling here, price against the closings. If you are buying, do not let the listing median anchor you. The gap between $308,000 and $420,000 is the gap between the market and the wish.

Is Sneads Ferry convenient to Camp Lejeune?

It is one of the closest civilian communities to the southern side of the installation, and Stone Bay is right there, which is why this town fills up with Marines. Sneads Ferry Gate and Wilson Gate are listed as open 24 hours. Treat that as a published schedule rather than a promise, because gates close for construction and for weather and the notices do not reach you before you are already in the car. The practical question for any house here is what your backup route looks like on the morning the gate you planned around is shut. If the answer is that you do not have one, keep looking.

Can I walk to the beach from Sneads Ferry?

No, and a lot of listings imply otherwise. This is a mainland fishing community on the New River. North Topsail Beach is across the bridge and it is a drive, not a walk. If beach access is the actual requirement, search the island and pay island prices and island insurance. If what you want is water, boats and a working coast twenty minutes from the sand, that is a different search and Sneads Ferry is very good at it. Deciding which of those two you are doing, before you start touring, saves more time than anything else on this page.

What should I ask about a waterfront listing here?

Exactly what conveys, in writing. A water view is not boat access. A shared community easement is not a private dock. A dock visible in the listing photograph may belong to the neighbor, and that is not always a mistake on the agent's part. Ask for the documents that define the access, any dues attached to it, and whether the arrangement transfers with the deed. This is the single most common disappointment in Sneads Ferry and it is entirely preventable with one question asked before the offer instead of after.

Should I pay extra for a Sneads Ferry house because it could be a rental?

Only if documented income supports it, and most of the time it does not. Being near the beach and near the base is a reason to investigate demand, not evidence of profit. Build the real model: realistic lease rate, financing, vacancy between tenants in a town where people rotate on orders, maintenance, management if you are not local, and the actual insurance quote on that address rather than last year's figure. Then check whether the community restricts rentals at all. If the asking price only works assuming the place never sits empty, the seller is asking you to carry that risk for them.

What is Sneads Ferry actually like?

A working fishing town that got discovered and has not finished deciding how it feels about that. Shrimp boats still come into the Fulchers Landing area, the Shrimp Festival is a genuine community event rather than something invented for tourists, and the Onslow County Environmental Education Center is a real Saturday for anyone with kids. Behind all of it, new construction goes up steadily. Both versions of this town are true at once right now, and which one you get depends heavily on which road you buy on.

Surf City, NC

Is a Surf City address on the island or the mainland?

Both exist, and this catches nearly every out of state buyer. Surf City includes island addresses on Topsail and mainland addresses across the bridge, same town name, entirely different purchase. It changes the price, the insurance, the resale pool and what your mornings look like. Settle this before you tour anything: do you want to walk to the ocean or drive to it? Everything else about the search follows from that one answer, and getting it wrong costs you either a hundred thousand dollars or the thing you moved here for.

How expensive is Surf City really?

Zillow put the median home value at $638,491 as of August 2026, against $437,413 for Pender County and $334,773 for North Carolina. That is roughly a 46 percent premium over the county, and you are paying it for location rather than for house. A modeled value is not a quote on an available property, so treat it as the frame and not the number. Then compare island comparables against island comparables. A countywide budget does not buy the same thing here that it buys twenty minutes inland, and the difference is not finishes.

Can insurance kill a Surf City deal?

Yes, and it is the most common way these purchases fall apart. North Carolina's Rate Bureau requested a 99.4 percent increase on beach territory covering Brunswick, Carteret, New Hanover, Onslow and Pender. The Department of Insurance settled at 16 percent for 2025 and 15.9 percent for 2026. That is published state record. It is also a territory-level base rate change, not your premium, so get a real wind and flood quote on the specific address with the deductibles spelled out before you decide what to offer. The number that breaks Topsail purchases is almost never the purchase price.

What is there besides the beach?

The sound side, which most buyers never look at and which is often the better half of the island at six in the evening. Soundside Park gives you water without the ocean crowd. The Karen Beasley Sea Turtle Rescue and Rehabilitation Center is a working turtle hospital rather than a gift shop with a tank, and tour arrangements change so check before you drive over. Spend an afternoon on both sides before you choose a street. Sunrise and sunset are on opposite sides of a barrier island and people buy the wrong one all the time.

Island or mainland, if I cannot decide?

Buy the island when direct beach access is worth the carrying cost and the maintenance that salt air demands. Buy the mainland when the beach can be a destination rather than the view. The honest test is how often you will actually use it. Plenty of people buy the island address and then drive everywhere anyway, which is an expensive way to own a parking problem. For a full time household rather than a rental investor, the mainland usually wins on the numbers and loses on the feeling, and you are allowed to choose the feeling if you can carry it.

What do people underestimate about owning here?

Upkeep. Salt gets into everything, HVAC units corrode faster, decks and railings need attention on a schedule rather than when they look bad, and the windows and doors on an exposed elevation are a real line item. Budget maintenance as a recurring number rather than a surprise. Second thing: if rental income is part of your plan, get the current town and community restrictions in writing before you count a single booking. Those rules change and they change after people have already bought on the old assumption.

Second home or rental on the island, the questions change. The payment calculator handles the carrying cost, investor and 1031 support covers the rest, and how I represent buyers explains the due diligence I run on flood and rental rules before you sign.

Hubert, NC

What do homes cost in Hubert?

Resideline tracked 330 closed sales over six months at a $295,000 median. That is close to the Onslow County figure while sitting meaningfully nearer the water than most of the county, which is the whole argument for this town. The caution on that number is that it blends a waterfront property, an interior subdivision house and a place needing real work into one figure, and those are not the same purchase. Use it to set a search range, then price the actual house off comparable closed sales with similar access and similar condition.

Can I count on the TOP Gate if I buy here?

No, and this is the Hubert mistake. The TOP Gate runs along NC-172 near Lyman Road and comes out on Bear Creek Road right here, which is exactly why the town looks perfect on a map. It is on reduced hours with weekend and holiday closures, and those hours have been modified more than once. Do not take a schedule from any directory, including this page. Confirm current access with the installation. Then plan the house around the route that exists when TOP is closed, because if you pull duty or work a weekend, that is your real commute.

Does a house near Great Neck Boat Ramp come with water access?

No. Great Neck Boat Ramp on Red Barn Road is a public launch and living near it gives you convenience, not rights. Ask whether the listing includes deeded water access, a community arrangement with dues, or simply a short drive to a public ramp. Those three things sit at very different prices. Then settle where the trailer lives, because plenty of neighborhoods here restrict it and people discover that after closing. Boat friendly is advertising until somebody shows you the document.

Is Hubert cheaper Swansboro, or something else?

Something else, and treating it as discount Swansboro leads to disappointment. You get bigger lots, older stock mixed with newer builds, genuine water access nearby and a quieter road. You do not get a walkable historic downtown with restaurants at the end of the street, and no Hubert address will give you that. Swansboro is close enough for dinner. If the thing you actually want is to walk to dinner, buy in Swansboro and pay for it. If you want land and a boat ramp and a shorter drive to the back of base, this is the better buy.

What is it like here day to day?

Quiet, strung along NC-24 and NC-172 between Jacksonville and Swansboro. The coastal waterways shape the appeal and the routine is built around driving, because there is no town center to walk to. Jacksonville handles the errands, Swansboro handles the evening out. If you want sidewalks, a coffee shop and neighbors you see without planning it, this is not that place and you will feel it by month three. If you want space, a shop, somewhere to put the boat and no traffic on your road, it is hard to beat at this price.

What should I verify before buying in Hubert?

Three things. Whether the parcel is on municipal water and sewer or on well and septic, because a lot of this inventory is the latter and the septic inspection is not optional. The flood determination on the actual parcel, since proximity to the waterways cuts both ways. And the real drive from that driveway to your authorized gate at the hour you would drive it, not the distance to the fence line. A Hubert mailing address covers a lot of ground and the parcel decides everything.

Hampstead, NC

What does a house in Hampstead cost?

Homes.com shows a median around $499,900, against a Pender County value of $437,413 from Zillow. Those two figures are different measures from different sources, so the gap between them is not a precise Hampstead premium and anyone presenting it that way is overreaching. What the numbers do establish is that Hampstead sits above its county, and the reasons are water access and the pull toward Wilmington. Narrow it from there with closed sales for the specific section and property type you can actually use.

Is Hampstead the right answer for a split commute?

It is close to the only town around here that seriously solves the problem, and a geographic midpoint can still give both people a bad drive. One person heading toward Wilmington, the other toward Jacksonville or the base, and US-17 is the whole equation. Put both destinations on the same map including the authorized gate, then drive both at the real hours. Start with whichever schedule is less flexible, because that is the one that cannot absorb a bad day. The common mistake is optimizing the easy commute while the hard one quietly becomes a daily tax on the other person.

Is insurance cheaper here than on Topsail?

Usually, and the territory still has to be confirmed on the specific address. The Department of Insurance settled eastern coastal base rate increases at 10.5 percent for 2025 and 10.1 percent for 2026, against a 71.4 percent request. Beach territory in the same counties settled at 16 and 15.9 percent. Those are different buckets and a Hampstead address can fall in either depending on where it sits. A soundside or creekfront quote and a quote a mile inland come back as different numbers entirely. Get the real one before you build a budget around the mortgage payment.

What is here besides a route to somewhere else?

More than the drive-through impression suggests. Hampstead Kiwanis Park on Sloop Point Loop Road is the local anchor for sports and outdoor recreation, there is real seafood that locals rather than tourists are eating, and the waterways are immediately accessible. The golf and soundside communities each run their own amenity structures with their own dues, so ask what is included and what is billed separately before the community sells you on the lifestyle.

When is Hampstead the wrong choice?

When you want to walk to the ocean. This is a mainland community and no Hampstead address delivers sand at the end of the street. If a listing is appealing mainly because it says near Topsail, look hard at the trip you are actually buying, because near and outside the door are different products at different prices. Buy Hampstead for a routine that works on the mainland and treat beach days as planned outings. People who get that backwards sell within three years.

What should I compare before writing an offer?

The exact address against your actual week, not the town against an idea. Drive both commutes at real hours. If the listing leans on boating, golf or community amenities, get what is included, what costs extra and what access attaches to the deed. Verify school assignment with Pender County by address rather than assuming it from the community name, because it is assigned address by address and it moves. Then put the home's condition and the recurring costs beside the location benefits and see whether the number still works.

Hampstead pulls toward Wilmington, so the commute math matters more than the price. The neighborhood matcher weighs that against the other towns. From there, buying or selling.

Holly Ridge, NC

Is Holly Ridge a cheaper way to live near Topsail?

It is the mainland alternative and the gap is real, with a caveat on how it is measured. Redfin shows $350,000 for Holly Ridge for the three months ending September 2026. Zillow's August 2026 value for Surf City is $638,491. Those are different metrics over different windows, so treat the difference as a direction rather than an exact discount. What you are buying is minutes from the Surf City bridge on US-17, without island pricing and without island insurance. What you are giving up is walking to the sand.

Why do the price numbers for Holly Ridge contradict each other?

Because a town this heavy with new construction swings on what happens to close in a given quarter. Redfin: $350,000, down 14.6 percent, three months ending September 2026. Zillow: $346,795, up 2.4 percent. Realtor.com: $380,000 listing median. Resideline: $380,000 across 205 closed sales over twelve months. Those do not reconcile into a single trend and averaging them would hide the reason. A quarter weighted toward smaller new builds drags the median down without any individual house losing value. Anyone telling you Holly Ridge dropped fifteen percent is reading a mix shift as a crash.

Should I wait for prices to fall further?

A falling town median is a weak reason to wait, because it may not be describing the house you want. Look at the competing listings and the closed sales that actually resemble your target, then decide. Waiting makes sense when nothing available fits your budget or your requirements. It does not make sense as a bet on a trend line built from a mix of new construction closings. Meanwhile you are paying rent and the inventory turns over.

Is Holly Ridge the same life as Surf City?

No, and the difference is bigger than the drive. Holly Ridge puts Topsail into a mainland routine, with beach trips you plan. Surf City puts the ocean into the structure of the day. Both are legitimate. For a full time household rather than a rental investor, the mainland version usually wins on the numbers and plenty of people are happier for it. Decide which one you are actually buying before you compare price per square foot, because the two are not competing products.

Does Holly Ridge have anything of its own?

A history most people drive straight past. The town grew up around Camp Davis during the Second World War, and that is a real identity rather than a subdivision name someone invented in 2019. Give a scouting trip a lunch stop and a slow drive through town before you continue to the beach. You are deciding whether you want a mainland base with island outings planned around it, and that question is easier to answer standing in the town than reading about it.

What should I watch buying here?

The builder down the street. New neighborhoods go up steadily, which means your eventual resale competes against a builder who can offer incentives you cannot match and a house nobody has lived in. If you are buying new, ask what is already finished and unsold, because that is where the concessions are. If you are buying resale, price against the builder's current offering rather than against what your street sold for last spring.

Most of what sells here is new construction, which has its own contract and timeline traps. Read new construction and builder representation before you walk into a model home alone, run the payment calculator, then see how I represent buyers.

Swansboro, NC

How much does a house cost in Swansboro?

Realtor.com shows a $454,000 median listing price and about 53 days on market. Among the twelve towns on this page, that is the highest listing median in Onslow County, and it is scarcity rather than hype. The historic waterfront is small, tightly held, and it almost never comes up. The caution is that an asking-price median is where sellers start, not where deals land, and this particular median is averaging two markets that have very little to do with each other.

Can I walk downtown from any Swansboro house?

No, and this is where the median misleads. Downtown Swansboro on the White Oak is one market, priced on charm and scarcity and walkability. The newer neighborhoods inland off NC-24 are a different market and they trade much closer to the county figure. A Swansboro mailing address establishes neither walkability nor water access. Map the actual walk from the actual property, crossings included, before you accept a downtown photograph as evidence. If walking to the waterfront is why you are buying here, make it a search requirement rather than a hope.

Is Swansboro a beach town?

It is a waterfront town, which is not the same thing, and the distinction costs people money. The White Oak River, the town docks and Hammocks Beach State Park shape the outdoor life here. The Atlantic is a separate trip. If you want a harbor, boats arriving at the dock and dinner a short walk from the water, Swansboro is excellent and genuinely rare on this coast. If you want to walk onto an ocean beach, you want the island and you should be looking there instead.

Is the downtown premium worth paying?

It is worth it if you will use the downtown, and a poor trade if it is mostly a photograph in the listing. Compare the real walking route, the parking situation, the condition of the house and what the maintenance looks like on an older structure, against an inland alternative at a lower number. The historic setting does not fix an aging system or create storage the house does not have. If the plan involves driving to nearly everything anyway, look hard at the premium before you agree to it.

What is a normal weekend here?

Downtown, mostly. The shops and restaurants sit right on the harbor, Bicentennial Park gives you White Oak views without owning a boat, and the town docks mean people arrive by water and walk up into the historic district, which is a genuinely good thing to watch on a Saturday. Hammocks Beach State Park is the bigger outing and it is reached by ferry, so access and schedules change and should be confirmed before you build a day around it.

What should I settle before buying here?

Whether you want to be inside the walkable core or outside it, because that is not a small difference in experience or in price. If a listing advertises water access, get the documents that define it and the costs attached. If you commute toward Jacksonville or the base, drive NC-24 at your actual hour before the harbor view does your thinking for you. And on an older downtown property, give the inspection the same attention you are giving the millwork.

Selling in Swansboro is a small-inventory game and pricing is most of it. The net proceeds calculator gives you the number, my listing approach explains the strategy, and if you are coming the other way, how I represent buyers.

Richlands, NC

What do homes cost in Richlands?

Realtor.com shows a $315,000 median listing price and about 57 days on market. Slower than the coast, which is the trade you are making. Use that as a search reference and then separate ordinary subdivision houses from acreage properties, because those two things wear the same town name and behave completely differently. An asking-price median tells you where sellers are starting. A house with land carries costs and limitations that no townwide figure will reveal.

Does a Richlands address mean more land for less money?

Not automatically, and assuming it is how buyers get burned here. Plenty of Richlands listings are ordinary houses on ordinary lots at ordinary prices. If you want acreage, a shop or room for equipment, search for those specifically and then look at usable space rather than total acres. A ten acre parcel with eight acres of wetland is a two acre homesite with a large tax bill. Get the survey, the access detail and a clear picture of where the site constraints sit before you treat land as buildable.

Should well and septic scare me off?

No, but unknown condition should slow you down. Confirm which systems actually serve the parcel, get the available permits and records, and arrange the right inspections rather than the generic ones. Then compare the permitted use against how you intend to use the house, because a listing advertising five bedrooms does not mean the septic is permitted for five. System age tells you more about your next five years than the kitchen does. A lower asking price gets less attractive fast when the system underneath it needs replacing.

Why choose Richlands over somewhere closer to the beach?

Because the inland setting and the property matter more to you than frequent ocean trips, and that is a legitimate choice rather than a compromise. This is north Onslow County off NC-24 and US-258, surrounded by farmland, with a historic downtown and the Onslow County Museum giving the town a center of its own. Plenty of people do the Jacksonville commute for a year and then decide fifteen extra minutes is worth half an acre and a shop. If your real priority is a spontaneous walk on the sand, buying here turns that into a planned trip.

What is actually in Richlands?

A small downtown with older buildings worth a slow drive, the Onslow County Museum, which is a better stop than people expect and whose hours are worth checking before you make the drive, and farmland in every direction. That is the honest inventory. There is no waterfront, no nightlife and no walkable shopping district. What there is instead is room, quiet and a price per acre that does not exist twenty minutes east.

What do buyers miss out here?

Internet. Service maps lie by neighborhood in rural Onslow County and the only reliable check is the exact address, so if you work from home, confirm it before due diligence runs out. Second, the drive. Richlands to Jacksonville looks short and the hour you travel changes it substantially. Third, outbuildings. A shop or barn in the photographs may have been built without permits, and that becomes your problem at resale rather than the seller's.

Land and acreage up here trade differently from houses. See inherited, land and problem properties for how I handle due diligence, or get a straight cash offer if you own something out here you want gone. The neighborhood matcher is a decent place to start if you are still comparing towns.

Maysville, NC

What do homes cost in Maysville?

Zillow put the median home value at $241,463 as of September 2026, up 5.2 percent. Realtor.com shows a $329,900 listing median, which tells you the handful of homes actively for sale are not representative of the housing stock. Jones County overall came in at $234,000 per Redfin for the three months ending September 2026. This is one of the lower entry points in the area, though not categorically the lowest, since Beulaville's estimated values run under it. Compare actual houses rather than ranking towns by headline.

Could a Maysville house have trouble appraising?

It can, and the risk is property specific rather than townwide. In a market with few transactions, an appraiser has fewer recent, nearby, genuinely comparable sales to work with, and an unusual property makes that harder. Look at the recency, distance, condition and similarity of the available closed sales before you rely on a county median to defend a price. Buyers should know the gap is possible and plan for it in the contract. Sellers should expect to support the number with something better than a neighbor's opinion.

Is Maysville coastal living?

No. It is river and rural, and that is the appeal rather than a shortfall. The White Oak River runs through town, the Croatan National Forest is on the doorstep, and Frost Park handles the local recreation. If your weekends involve a paddle, a dog and a truck, this town makes a lot more sense than its price suggests. If what you actually want is the beach at an inland price, be honest about how often that drive would happen, because the house has to work on the days it does not.

What makes a cheap Maysville house expensive?

Condition, access and operating costs, in that order. The purchase price and the cost of making the property do what you want are different numbers and the gap can be large out here. Add the documented condition of the home and its systems, internet availability at that exact address, and the travel the location commits you to every week. A low town value does not produce a low monthly obligation. The attractive entry price is where the investigation starts, not where it ends.

How far is everything from Maysville?

Depends entirely on which direction you are going, and that is the point. Maysville sits between Jacksonville and New Bern, with Cherry Point a third answer again. Pick your real destination, drive it once at your real hour, and then decide whether the distance is acceptable. Usually it is. Occasionally it very much is not, and the people who skip that drive are the ones who list again in two years.

Who is Maysville genuinely right for?

Someone buying land, someone who wants quiet on purpose, and someone whose work either travels or happens at home with verified internet. It also suits a buyer who has been outbid repeatedly closer in and wants to stop competing. It does not suit a household that needs restaurants, a short school run and a predictable commute to one fixed office. Being clear about which of those you are is worth more than any price comparison on this page.

Beulaville, NC

What does a house cost in Beulaville?

Zillow shows a median home value of $221,690 as of September 2026, up 3.4 percent. Homes.com reports a $286,400 median sale price, down 5 percent year over year, at about $173 a foot. Duplin County overall ran $288,000 per Redfin for the three months ending September 2026. Those measure different things, and picking whichever headline flatters the argument is how both buyers and sellers end up wrong. Evaluate an actual house against actual closed comparables. No headline on this page establishes the condition of the property in front of you.

Why buy inland when the coast is an hour away?

Because the distance is the product, not the penalty. Duplin County is agricultural, quiet, and priced like it. Cabin Lake County Park north of town gives the area camping, fishing, paddling and a trail around the lake, which is a real outdoor life that has nothing to do with the ocean. The test is honest: if the household needs frequent beach access or a demanding base commute, the lower price has to cover the travel, and a smaller purchase figure does not shorten anybody's workday.

Does rental math work better here than on the coast?

It can, and it has to be underwritten rather than assumed. The entry price supports numbers that stopped working near the water two years ago, largely because insurance has not hit inland Duplin the way it has hit the beach territories. Start from supported local rent rather than imported beach-rental assumptions, then subtract vacancy, repairs, management if you are not local, insurance, taxes and financing. Include whatever the property needs before a tenant can move in. A house can look inexpensive and still return badly.

Would you buy my Beulaville house instead of listing it?

Possibly. I buy houses, land and mobile homes for my own account, and that is a different transaction from representing you, with different math and different disclosures. If a direct purchase makes sense for your situation, you see that number next to what a listing would realistically net after time on market, repairs and carrying costs, and you choose. What I will not do is pretend a cash number matches a retail sale. It does not, and the speed and certainty are what you are trading for.

What should I verify before buying out here?

Usable acreage rather than total, legal access to the parcel, the condition and permitting of any outbuildings, and the utility arrangement, meaning well and septic in most cases with the inspection that goes with it. Then internet at the exact address if your work depends on it, because rural coverage varies street by street and the provider maps are optimistic. Expect the same thin comparable sales issue Maysville has, which cuts both ways at appraisal.

Who should not buy in Beulaville?

Anyone who needs restaurants, a short commute to a fixed office, or frequent coastal access. That is not a knock on the town, it is a description of it, and knowing it now saves you a bad two years. Beulaville suits people who want distance deliberately and investors who can make the rent math work on a verified basis. Nobody ends up here by accident, and the ones who are happiest chose it on purpose.

Acreage and older farmhouses are most of what moves here. For an inherited place or anything that needs work, inherited, land and problem properties explains the process, and a cash offer alongside a list price lets you compare instead of guessing. Buyers, start here.

New Bern, NC

What do homes cost in New Bern?

It depends enormously on where, and the sources measure different things. Zillow shows a $320,833 median sale as of August 2026 against a $283,192 modeled value. Resideline puts the median closed price at $333,860. The Historic District is a separate market at roughly $642,000 per Redfin with homes sitting around 101 days. Do not let a downtown comparable set your expectation for a house three miles out, and do not let the citywide figure set your expectation downtown. They describe different purchases.

Is New Bern a river town or a beach town?

A river city, and conflating the two leads people to the wrong search. The Neuse and the Trent meet here and the water is the whole identity, but the Atlantic is a separate trip entirely. Union Point Park sits right where the rivers come together and makes the geography obvious in about ten minutes. Tryon Palace and the North Carolina History Center give downtown genuine historical weight rather than a preserved main street. If you want to live beside the ocean, New Bern is solving a different problem.

Why are historic New Bern homes so much more expensive?

Because the Historic District is a distinct segment with limited supply, not because every old house in the city commands that price. Redfin's figure for the district sits near $642,000 with roughly 101 days on market, which tells you these are slower, higher-priced, more particular sales. Character also comes with obligations. Look hard at condition, repair history and what you are permitted to change on the exterior if the property sits under review requirements. Appearance cannot establish the cost of maintaining it.

What should I know about buying an older home here?

Give the inspection the same attention you are giving the millwork. Historic houses carry systems, foundations and repair histories that do not photograph, and the upkeep is permanent rather than a phase. Ask for records of what has been replaced and when. Budget for the trades who actually work on this kind of house, which is a smaller and more expensive list than the general contractor pool. And if there are exterior review requirements, read them before you buy the place planning to change it.

Should a Jacksonville broker handle a New Bern search?

Sometimes not, and you should hear that before you sign anything. New Bern is about an hour from Jacksonville and I work it and refer into it in both directions. If your search is centered here and the property is a historic district house needing deep local comparables and review knowledge, ask me directly how it would be handled and who would be involved. If the honest answer is that someone working those streets daily serves you better, I will say so and make the introduction.

When is New Bern the right call?

When you want a real downtown, water that is part of daily life rather than a weekend, and housing stock with actual history, and when you are comparing that against Jacksonville or the coast rather than against the beach. It also works for a Cherry Point commute, which is a different map from a Lejeune commute and worth driving before you decide. Spend an afternoon on foot downtown before you tour a single house. It will tell you quickly whether this is your kind of place.

Topsail Beach, NC

Is Topsail Beach the same as Topsail Island?

No, and searching as though it is wastes weeks. Topsail Beach is a town at the southern end of the island in Pender County. The island also contains Surf City and North Topsail Beach. Three towns, three price points, three sets of local rules, one name on the signs. Zillow put Topsail Beach's median value at $947,446 as of September 2026, up 4.7 percent, while Redfin shows North Topsail Beach at $575,000 for September 2026. Same island, different markets, different measures. Establish the municipality before you evaluate anything.

What should I budget for a Topsail Beach home?

Start with the carrying cost and work backward to the purchase price, because doing it the other way around is how people talk themselves into a number that does not exist. Realtor.com shows a $1.3 million listing median. That figure says nothing about what owning the house costs. Get financing, taxes, insurance with real deductibles, maintenance on an exposed coastal structure, and any association costs before you compare a listing to a budget. The question is whether you can carry it comfortably through a season with no rental income.

How much are insurance rates going up here?

The Department of Insurance approved beach territory base rate increases of 16 percent for 2025 and 15.9 percent for 2026, after the Rate Bureau requested 99.4 percent. Those are territory-level base rate changes and they are not your premium, and they should not be added together and described as a compounded increase. Get the actual coverage cost, exclusions and deductibles on the specific address. NC DEQ has also published a resilience strategy for Topsail Island that is worth reading before you buy, because it describes the exposure in plainer terms than any listing will.

Should I trust a listing that says rentals will cover the mortgage?

No, not without records. Ask for documented rental performance rather than a projection, plus the owner-use assumptions baked into it, the management fees, maintenance, vacancy and the current town and community restrictions. Then compare net income against the full carrying cost including that insurance quote. Restrictions change, and they change after people have already bought on the old rules. If the purchase only works on optimistic bookings, the seller is handing you the downside and keeping the price.

What is the south end actually like?

Quieter than Surf City and much quieter than it looks in July. Mostly second homes and rentals with a small year-round population, which means winter here is genuinely empty and some people love that and some discover they do not. The Missiles and More Museum in the old Assembly Building covers Operation Bumblebee and the island's strange military history, and it is a better hour than you would expect. Walk the route from the house to the public access you would really use before you decide.

Oceanfront or soundfront?

Different lives, and the price does not always tell you which one you are getting. Oceanfront gives you the surf, the rental demand and the maintenance, with wind and salt working on the structure constantly. Soundfront gives you calmer water, better boat access, sunsets rather than sunrises, and usually a lower insurance picture. Spend an evening on both sides before you commit. The number of buyers who purchase the wrong side of a barrier island because they only visited at eleven in the morning is higher than anyone admits.

Almost everything down here is a second home or a rental, which makes it an investment decision first. See investors, flips and 1031, run the carrying cost through the payment calculator, then how I represent buyers.
