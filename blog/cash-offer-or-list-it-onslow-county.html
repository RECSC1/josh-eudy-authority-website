Cash Offer or List It? How to Compare Both Numbers on Your Onslow County House

By Josh Eudy·October 7, 2026·Selling

Most sellers only ever see one number. A cash buyer shows you theirs, or an agent shows you theirs, and you are left guessing what the other one would have been. Here is how to put both side by side.

The short answer

Take a cash offer when the house needs major repairs, you need a certain closing date, or a long listing would cost you more in payments and fixes than it would earn. List it when the house is in good shape and you have a few months. The only way to know is to compare the cash number against what you would actually net after listing, not against the list price.

I am in an unusual spot. I am a licensed broker who lists houses, and I am also a buyer who purchases houses with my own money. So when a seller asks me whether they should take a cash offer or put the house on the market, I can give them both numbers. Most people never get that. They talk to a cash buyer who only buys, or an agent who only lists, and each one has a reason to steer.

This is how I walk sellers through the comparison. You can use it with me or with anyone.

Why this question matters more in 2026

The Jacksonville market has shifted toward buyers. Redfin data for the first half of 2026 shows active listings up 21.8 percent over last year, homes taking about 60 days to sell, roughly 28 days longer than a year ago, and about 3.5 months of supply. Prices have held, with a median sale price around $312,800, up 4 percent. In plain English: houses still sell, but they sit longer, and every extra month on the market is another month of payments, insurance and utilities you are covering. That changes the math on a cash offer for a lot of sellers.

What does a cash offer actually get you?

A cash offer is not just a price. It is a package of things you no longer have to deal with:

  • No repairs. The buyer takes the house as is, including the roof, the HVAC and whatever the inspector would have found.
  • No showings. No keeping the house spotless for strangers on a Saturday.
  • No financing risk. No lender, no appraisal, and far less chance the deal falls apart three days before closing.
  • Your timeline. Close in a couple of weeks, or pick a date that works around a move, an estate or a set of orders.

The trade is price. A cash buyer is taking on the repairs, the holding costs and the risk, so the offer will be lower than what a fixed-up house would sell for on the open market. The real question is whether it is lower than what you would actually walk away with after listing.

What does listing your house really cost?

The list price is the number everyone looks at. It is not the number that hits your bank account. Before you compare, subtract these:

  • Repairs and prep. Whatever it takes to get the house ready to show, plus whatever the buyer's inspection turns up and asks you to fix.
  • The listing fee you agree to with your agent.
  • Carrying costs. Every month on the market, you are still paying the mortgage, taxes, insurance, utilities and lawn care.
  • Concessions. Closing cost help or repair credits that buyers ask for, especially on older houses.
  • The fall-through risk. If a buyer walks during due diligence, you start the clock over.

North Carolina excise tax, payoff of any liens and your closing attorney's fees apply either way, so they do not change the comparison.

The side by side

ItemCash offerListing
PriceLower, as isHigher, after prep
Repairs before sellingNoneUsually some
Repairs after inspectionNoneOften negotiated
Months of carrying costsLittle to noneDepends on the market
Chance the deal falls throughLowReal, especially with financing
Closing dateYour choiceThe buyer's lender decides

When you fill that in with real numbers for your house, the gap between the two options is often smaller than people expect. Sometimes listing wins by a lot. Sometimes it does not win at all.

When does a cash offer make sense?

  • The house needs more work than you have the time or money to do.
  • You inherited it and the family wants it handled, not managed.
  • You are behind on payments and a long listing could cost you more than it earns.
  • There is a tenant situation you do not want to work around.
  • You are on orders, or a job is moving you, and the calendar matters more than the last dollar.
  • It is land, a lot or a mobile home, where buyer financing can be harder to line up.

If any of those sound like you, ask me for a cash number and I will put it next to a listing estimate.

When does listing win?

If the house is in good shape, you have a few months, and it sits in a price band where buyers are active, listing almost always nets more. If that is your situation, I will tell you so, and I will list it. Start with a real price range and see the listing plan.

One thing you should always get in writing

When I make a cash offer, I am the buyer, not your agent. I tell every seller that in writing, along with the fact that I am a licensed broker, before anything is signed. You are always free to have your own agent or attorney look at the numbers. If a buyer will not let you do that, that tells you something.
